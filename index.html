What Is High Pressure Hydro Jetting?

Hydro jetting is a drain cleaning method that sends water through a specialized nozzle at very high pressure. The nozzle fires jets backward to pull the hose forward and sideways to scour the pipe wall in every direction. Because the water contacts the entire circumference of the pipe, it removes the layered deposits that cause the same clog to return a few weeks after a basic snaking visit.

The technique works on kitchen lines, bathroom branch lines, main sewer lines, and large commercial drains. It uses water alone, with no harsh chemicals, which makes it a cleaner choice for your plumbing and for the surrounding soil and landscaping.

How the Hydro Jetting Process Works

A well-run job follows a clear sequence. Skipping any stage increases the chance of damage or an incomplete result.

1. Camera Inspection

A waterproof camera travels through the line first. This reveals the pipe material, the type of blockage, and any cracks, offsets, or collapsed sections. Jetting a damaged pipe without knowing it is exposed can make the damage worse, so this step protects your plumbing.

2. Pressure Selection and Jetting

The technician matches nozzle type and pressure to the pipe. Cast iron, clay, PVC, and ABS each tolerate different forces. The hose then advances slowly through the line while the jets cut grease, scale, and roots away from the walls and flush the debris downstream.

3. Verification Pass

After jetting, the camera runs again to confirm the pipe is clear and to document the condition of the line. This footage is useful for future maintenance planning and for any later repair decisions.

Signs Your Sherman Oaks Drain Needs Hydro Jetting

Many blockages announce themselves long before a full backup. Watch for these warning signs:

  • Water draining slowly in more than one fixture at the same time
  • Gurgling sounds from toilets or tubs after other fixtures run
  • Persistent sewer odors indoors or near cleanout access points
  • Clogs that return soon after being snaked
  • Wet or unusually green patches in the yard above the sewer line
  • Repeated backups in a restaurant, salon, or multi-unit building

If several of these apply, the problem is probably deeper than a single clog, and a full-wall cleaning is the more reliable response.

Hydro Jetting vs. Traditional Drain Snaking

Both methods have a place, but they solve different problems. A snake, or auger, breaks through a blockage and restores flow quickly. Jetting removes the material itself.

Why Sherman Oaks Properties Face Recurring Clogs

Many established San Fernando Valley neighborhoods contain older homes with original cast iron, clay, or Orangeburg sewer lines. These materials develop rough interior surfaces over decades, which catch grease, hair, and paper. Mature trees add another factor, since roots seek out moisture and enter through small joints. The result is a slow buildup that eventually restricts the pipe on its own schedule.

Commercial properties face a different challenge. Restaurants and food service businesses send fats, oils, and grease into their lines every day. Without regular cleaning, that material hardens into thick deposits that standard tools cannot fully remove.

Is Hydro Jetting Safe for My Pipes?

For sound pipes, yes. A trained technician adjusts pressure to the pipe material and age, and the camera inspection confirms the line can handle the work. Pipes that are cracked, collapsed, or badly misaligned are different. In those cases, the better path is to repair or replace the damaged section first, then clean the line. A reputable provider will tell you this before starting rather than after.

Residential and Commercial Uses

Homeowners use hydro jetting for main line backups, slow kitchen drains, and lines affected by roots. Property managers use it to keep multi-unit buildings flowing and to cut emergency calls. Restaurants, cafes, and commercial kitchens schedule it as routine grease control. Each setting benefits from the same principle: clean the whole pipe, then maintain it on a schedule that fits the load placed on it.

How Often Should You Schedule Hydro Jetting?

Frequency depends on use. Most homes do well with a cleaning every 18 to 24 months, especially older properties with mature trees nearby. Restaurants often need service every three to six months, while lightly used commercial lines may need it less often. After your first visit, your camera footage gives the clearest guide for setting an interval that prevents buildup without over-servicing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will hydro jetting damage old pipes?

When pressure is matched to pipe material and condition, it is safe. Severely cracked or collapsed pipes should be repaired first.

How often should I schedule hydro jetting?

Homes typically benefit every 18 to 24 months, and restaurants every 3 to 6 months.
